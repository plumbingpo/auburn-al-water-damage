Where Water Damage Starts in Montgomery Properties

Starting at the top of a home and working toward the ground is a handy way to think about water damage. That is the route we take when inspecting a property in Montgomery, and five spots along it account for most calls.

Attics: Rain that slips past a damaged shingle or a loose vent boot lands on the attic floor first. Insulation acts like a sponge, so it can hold a surprising amount before any of it shows on the ceiling. Once soaked, that material no longer does its job and holds moisture against the ceiling joists. We take out the ruined material, dry the framing and leave the roof repair itself to a roofer.

Bathrooms: Bathroom floods usually come from a forgotten bathtub, a stopped-up sink or a toilet that will not quit rising. Tile looks waterproof, yet water gets through grout lines and around the edges of the room. In an upstairs bathroom the next stop is the ceiling of the room underneath. If the toilet bowl was the source, assume the water is unsanitary and have the area disinfected.

Walls: Plumbing hidden in a wall may seep at a joint for weeks without leaving a puddle anywhere. The hints are small: a baseboard that swells, a stain that grows, or a patch of wall that feels cool and soft. On these calls in Montgomery, an infrared camera and a pin meter show us how far the dampness reaches. After the pipe is repaired by a plumber, we open just the damp part and dry the cavity.

Tanks: The hot water tank sits out of sight in a garage, closet or utility space and stays full all the time. Drips from the relief valve, the drain valve or the fittings on top are early signs that parts are wearing. If the tank lets go, shut the cold inlet valve over the heater and turn off its power or gas supply. Our Auburn Restoration crew extracts the water and checks the nearby walls and flooring, which often get wet from behind.

Drains: The lowest drain in the house is where a sewer blockage shows itself. If murky water comes up in a downstairs shower or floor drain, waste has nowhere to go but back inside. That water is Category 3, the most contaminated kind, and it takes protective equipment to clean up safely. Stop using water in the house and keep people and pets away until a trained crew arrives in Montgomery.

How Drying Works on a Montgomery Water Damage Job

A wet building left alone does dry eventually, but far too slowly. Moisture inside walls, studs and subfloors can linger for weeks, and that is plenty of time for mold to grow and wood to distort. On each Montgomery job, our task is to make that happen quickly and under control.

First the liquid water comes out, because extraction removes far more water per hour than evaporation ever will. Then air movers are set along the walls to push a fast stream of air across wet surfaces and lift moisture out of them. That vapor is collected by dehumidifiers and pumped away, keeping the air dry so the process does not stall. Without the dehumidifiers, the air movers would just move damp air around.

A technician measures the wet materials daily against readings from an unaffected area of the property. For most Montgomery homes the target is met in 3 to 5 days, though hardwood, plaster and other dense materials need more time. We remove the machines when the readings reach the goal, and not before.

How Montgomery Homeowners Can Avoid Water Damage

A flood in the house feels like it came from nowhere, yet most trace back to wear that built up over months or years. Knowing the usual suspects is most of the prevention for a Montgomery homeowner.

Plumbing comes first. Watch your water bill and your water pressure, because an unexplained rise in either can point to a hidden leak or stress on the pipes. Appliances are second. Run the dishwasher and the washer only while someone is in the house, and change their hoses before they show cracks. The roof and the ground outside are third. Rain gets in through damaged roofing, failed flashing, clogged gutters and ground that tilts toward the walls.

Going away for more than a few days? Turn the water off at the main, or ask someone to check the house. Homeowners in Montgomery can ask Auburn Restoration about any of this. And if the worst happens anyway, a fast call keeps a small leak from turning into a large repair.
